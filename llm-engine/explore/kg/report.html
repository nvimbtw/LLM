E4 — A knowledge-graph view of the corpus

llm-engine/explore/kg · e4_graph.py · corpus word-graph vs the trained model's embedding space

What was done

The favourite workhorse here is the user's knowledge graph idea. A transformer learns from token streams; a knowledge graph would teach it the entity & relation structure. Before proposing any graph machinery inside the model, we measured how much graph-like structure the corpus already carries and whether the trained model's embeddings already agree with it:

  • a weighted word co-occurrence graph (per-document windows, edges if two frequent words appear within 5 of each other, weight=n);
  • degree/hubs, page-rank, clustering, connected components, communities;
  • whether graph neighbours are closer than non-neighbours in the embedding space (cosine of BPE-averaged word vectors), and how similarity decays with hop distance;
  • whether graph communities separate as clusters of embeddings;
  • the BPE "cost" of entity-like (capitalised) phrases, and how often they recur across documents.

The key number: the model's embeddings pick up the co-occurrence graph weakly and only for hop 1. Hop-2 neighbours are indistinguishable from disconnected token pairs (0.0172 each) — i.e. the model has essentially no relational generalisation over the graph: it remembers direct in-window co-occurrence, nothing longer.

Interesting / worrying

  • The corpus graph is basically one giant clique of function words (degree ~300 for all)/paragraph. Content words nest inside it. Pagerank is dominated by generic fillers (world, rate) — raw text has no notion of "entity or topic".
  • Communities do separate in embedding space (intra 0.026 vs inter 0.012) — but both values are tiny, and the separation is small relative to the anisotropy floor from E3. A graph-aware model could multiply that margin.
  • Entity phrases recur overwhelmingly across documents (6,112 overlaps), yet cost 2–76 BPE pieces; that's the storage bill the model pays for "australia" (4 pieces) vs a single token a byte-level BPE could and should have.

Why it would be a good addition

This report is the argument for a graph-aware layer — the "knowledge-graph" addition the user cares about:

  • Entity-token prelexicon. Detect entities (the capitalised phrases, ~4k of them) at tokenization time and reserve the top ~512 as real single tokens (or an entity-code appended to the BPE). Directly removes the 2–76 piece tax: e.g. "australia" 4→1 token, "united states" 2–3→1.
  • Graph-quenched embeddings. Make word embeddings agree with the adjacency matrix via a cheap contrastive term over co-occurrence windows — the "agraph contrast" would widen the hop-1 gap and extend it to hop-2. One aux loss + one hook; no change to the forward pass.
  • A "graph memory" attention. A second attention block over entity embeddings (not tokens) injected as a residual — the model attends over concepts, then folds fact-set signals back into the token stream. This is the closest thing to "a knowledge graph inside the model" without loading an external KG.

Verdict: entity-prelexing is a pure pre-processing win you can land today; the embedding contrast term is the smallest true in-train change that would make these numbers move; the graph-memory block is the long idea worth a dedicated branch (see bottom of the training report).

How it changes the workflow

  • Vocabulary: entities become first-class tokens → token/EOT counts drop per document, and every repetition of "australia" saves tokens.
  • Dataset: an optional kg: step runs e4's graph build at ingest; graph metrics (hop-1 gap) get added as a diagnostic gauge so you can watch the model grow relational memory.
  • Training: the contrastive loss can be a regularizer flag in plan.py — off by default, so standard runs unaffected.
