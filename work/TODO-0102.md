## Description

The GPU queue and everything that drives it: `queue.rs` (1,467 lines, the async
task that owns the pool and is the only thing that starts a job),
`train_worker.rs` (745, the blocking training half and its telemetry), and
`cycle.rs` (636, the multi-round train/label/tune loop). 2,848 lines total.

**These three are one crate, not three, and that is forced.** `train_worker.rs`
imports `crate::queue::{ProgressTick, emit, frame, lock}`; `queue.rs` calls
`train_worker::{run, snapshot_frame}` and matches on `train_worker::Outcome`.
That is a genuine cycle, and Rust has no circular crate dependencies. Breaking
it would mean extracting the shared progress/telemetry types into a fourth
crate — a real design change, not a move, and explicitly out of scope here. If
it is worth doing it gets its own artifact.

`cycle.rs` depends on `queue`, `training`, `gather` and `db`, and nothing
depends on `cycle` except `routes/cycles.rs`, so it joins them without
introducing anything new.

Dependencies: `db`, `storage`, `training`, `synth`, `labeling`, plus `sqlx`
(`PgPool`), `tokio`, `serde_json`, `llm`. This is the first crate in the epic
that touches the database, which is why it lands after TODO-0098.

Note for the boundary: `backend/CLAUDE.md` states the invariant that
high-frequency frames (loss series, per-layer diagnostics) must **never** be
written per-frame to Postgres, and that live state lives in memory only while a
job runs. Moving to a crate does not change that, but the `pub` surface should
not start exposing `Queue::live` internals in a way that invites it.

## Definition of done

- `crates/jobs/` exists with `queue`, `train_worker` and `cycle` as modules;
  all three files are gone from `backend/src/` and their `mod` declarations are
  removed from `main.rs`.
- `grep -rn "crate::queue\|crate::train_worker\|crate::cycle" backend/src
  backend/crates` returns nothing.
- The crate's `Cargo.toml` names no `actix-web`.
- `cargo build` and `cargo clippy --all-targets` clean; `cargo test` passes.
- Start the app, queue a short training run, and confirm progress frames still
  arrive over the socket and the job reaches a terminal row — the queue is the
  one piece where a compile-clean move can still be behaviourally wrong.
