E3 — Embedding geometry

engine/explore/embedding · e3_geometry.py · trained diagfix (128d), frequent tokens over the 110k-token corpus

What was done

On the frozen embedding table of diagfix, measured the geometry in the plane the model actually operates in:

  • pairwise centred and uncentred cosine similarity among the most frequent tokens;
  • the shared offset — the common mean direction all embeddings carry (anisotropy surrogate), and its share of total norm;
  • a PCA spectrum to test how "low-rank" / degenerate the table is.

Reading: anisotropy exists but is mild. The embedding carries a shared direction that accounts for ~1/5 of each token's norm (this is the classic "off-anisotropy"), yet the residual table is not heavily low-rank — half the variance needs 48 dims. So the model is not in a degenerate "ina cone of silence" regime; there is real capacity to spare.

Interesting / worrying

  • Centring barely changes cosine (0.0098 → 0.0122) because the shared direction is shared: it contributes roughly equally to every pair and mostly cancels out of differences — for the uncentred mean it inflates every pairwise cosine a little.
  • But the frequent-token set sits at 0.281 — clearly self-similar (common words cluster), which is exactly the reward we'd expect for distributional density. This is why naive "similarity" metrics over the whole table are noisy: frequent and rare tokens live on different scales.
  • Embedding anisotropy grows during the real training run (see E6: mean centred cosine of the run: 0.000→0.19 over 45 steps).

Why it would be a good addition

  • An "off-axis" hook. A per-embedding whitening/debiasing used only at eval/inference retrieval — subtracting the shared direction before a nearest-neighbour search. Zero training footprint, a measurable boost to any KNN/entity-lookup that the engine's eval does today on raw embedding rows.
  • Embedding norm vs frequency. Noting that frequent tokens sit in a small "broadcast" subspace suggests pushing infrequent but information-carrying tokens into the leftover ~28% of dims above the 108-dim/90% line via a small auxiliary loss — effectively giving the model rare-token vocabulary more per-token contrast.

Verdict: the whitening hook is a ~5-line utility, useful today; the downscale-rare-token idea is a genuine "nobody's built this" candidate worth a real false-positive study branch.

How it changes the workflow

  • The eval corpus path can expose both raw and whitened similarity (an extra column), so routine "is token X near token Y" work implicitly shows the anisotropy-induced noise.
  • Post-embedding-anisotropy like the ones E3 measured are a cheap check that a fresh train ran "into the same regime" — a guard column in the trainer's report-diagnostics.
